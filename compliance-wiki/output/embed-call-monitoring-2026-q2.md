# Embed Call Monitoring Review — Q2 2026

**Document owner:** Compliance Team, [[Kota]]
**Reviewer:** Sola Olaniyan
**Date of review:** 12–13 August 2026
**Period covered:** June 2026 (Q2 2026)
**Source:** Google Drive — `Monitoring and Testing / Embed Call Monitoring` ([folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1pjUqYY0wZvdpe2dXT5hNH7m43JfZEmHL))
**Population:** 5 recordings
**Reviewed:** 5 of 5 — full coverage
**Classification:** Internal — Confidential

All five recordings have now been reviewed. Two Embed partnerships are covered: **Smart Pension** (three calls — pilot cohort, contractual terms, technical design) and **Justworks** (one call), plus one **Rivermate / Hightekers** EOR call. The Kota attendee on all five is John Higgins, joined on the technical session by Sahani, Trevor Gardiner, Deepak and Justin.

Forty-two findings, of which one is a non-compliance that has already occurred. The concentration is not in individual conduct on calls — it is that two insurance distribution arrangements were designed end to end across June, in commercial, technical and contractual detail, with **no compliance, legal, data protection or product governance involvement at any point in any of the five conversations**.

## Call — 10 June 2026 — Kota X Smart | Pilot Cohort & Success Criteria

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Date of call** | 10 June 2026, 09:00 UTC |
| **Duration** | 32 minutes |
| **Kota attendee** | John Higgins |
| **Counterparty** | Smart Pension — Rosie Crowley, James Briginshaw, Nikki Moss |

| **Consumer present** | No |
| **Recording consent requested** | **No** |

| **Overall rating** | **PC** |

### Purpose

Defining the cohort of Smart Pension employers for the initial 12-month Embed pilot, and the commercial success criteria that trigger continuation into years two and three. Insurance would be presented to, and taken out by, employers within Smart Pension's master trust book — described on the call as "insured through Smart, AKA Kota, embedded within Smart" ([04:39](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=279)).

### Scoping note — which conduct regime applies

Smart Pension is a UK master trust, the figures discussed were sterling, and the cohort is UK employers. The pilot therefore sits primarily in the **UK** perimeter (FCA — ICOBS, PROD, Consumer Duty, and Kota's appointed representative permissions), not only the Irish CBI/IDD framework. Findings cite both; the UK analysis should be treated as the operative one and confirmed with Legal. Regulatory references marked *(confirm)* need to be checked against the source instrument before they are relied on externally.

### What was done well

- Professional, straightforward tone throughout; no pressure tactics.
- **No advice or product recommendation given** — no [[Minimum Competency Code]] exposure on this call.
- Modelling assumptions were explicitly labelled as assumptions rather than presented as fact — "I'll list all the assumptions out" ([21:34](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=1294)).
- The pilot was framed as a reversible experiment with an explicit review gate.
- Selection bias was actively resisted when designing the cohort — Smart declined to cherry-pick on data quality "because... one of the learnings we want to have is actually like, can this work?" ([13:15](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=795)). Good testing discipline — though see **F33**, where it was reversed a week later.

### Findings

#### F1 — No consent requested before recording | Data Protection | PC
**Priority: Medium · Impact: Medium**

The call opens directly with greetings; consent to record is never requested, and the recording was subsequently processed by a third-party AI transcription provider (Fireflies) without notice to the three external participants.

- **Reference:** GDPR Art.6 (lawful basis), Art.13 (information at collection); [[Data Retention]] for retention of the resulting records.
- **Why it is escalating:** third consecutive quarter. Logged in Q1 2026 on 23/01/2026 ("No request to record the call. This will inhibit our ability to implement our AI processes involving customer biometric data") and 30/04/2026. Q1 rated it Low/Low; the repeat plus the AI-processing dimension justifies Medium/Medium.
- The register's Q1 summary records "Improvements in asking for consent to record the call" as a positive for customer-facing calls. The gap is specifically on **partner-facing** calls, which sit outside the sales script.

#### F2 — Volume-linked revenue share agreed in principle with no conflicts assessment | Conduct / Insurance Distribution | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: Medium**

The commercial structure is a commission share that **increases as insurance sales volume increases**: "your commission is 15 and then we get 50 of that" ([09:32](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=572)), rising on a sliding scale — "by year two... you hit certain volumes, it goes to 60 rev share to you guys, [70] the next one" ([24:11](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=1451); transcript renders this as "770").

A distribution arrangement that pays the introducing partner a **higher** share the more insurance is sold requires a documented conflicts-of-interest and remuneration assessment before it is contracted. Compliance holds none. See **F18** — five days later this became a contractual rate card.

- **Reference:** IDD Art.17(3); IDD Art.24; Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/2358 Art.8 and Art.10; FCA ICOBS 2.3 (inducements) and SYSC 10 *(confirm)*. See [[Insurance Distribution]], [[Product Governance]].

#### F3 — Target market absent from cohort design | Product Governance | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: Medium**

The cohort was defined entirely on commercial and operational grounds — employer size bands, back book versus net new, data quality, conversion modelling. The manufacturer's **target market** was never referenced. Distribution was then opened to all new business irrespective of size: "anyone coming in new can access this. So I don't really care about your company size. Let's just offer it across the board" ([15:34](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=934)).

- **Reference:** S.I. No. 229/2018, Reg.38(6)–(8); IDD Art.25(3)–(4); Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/2358 Art.10; FCA PROD 4.3 *(confirm)*. See [[Product Governance]].
- **Context:** EIOPA's 2023 POG peer review found target market definitions too broad to give distributors meaningful guidance, and distributor feedback loops weak — see [[EIOPA Peer Review on Product Oversight and Governance — 2023]]. POG supervision extends to all insurance products from 2026.

#### F4 — Change in data-sharing scope relied on second-hand privacy clearance | Data Protection | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: High**

The scope of member data available to the pilot was reported as widened by a privacy decision taken on a call Compliance was not party to: "you weren't kind of on the calls with Joanne in terms of privacy. They've confirmed that we can actually, based on kind of where they got to with the data sharing, we can include whoever we want. Now we're not tied to kind of new business or current back book" ([07:44](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=464)).

Smart's book was described as 2 million members of whom roughly 400,000 are active lives ([22:39](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=1359)), with the modelled pilot cohort at ~156,000 active lives. Presenting insurance to an existing pension back book is direct marketing to data subjects who joined for a different purpose. Compliance holds no record of the clearance, no data sharing agreement, no DPIA, and no confirmed lawful basis.

- **Reference:** GDPR Art.6, Art.13–14, Art.28/26, Art.35; UK PECR Reg.22 for the UK book, S.I. No. 336/2011 Reg.13 in Ireland *(confirm which applies per channel)*.

#### F5 — Smart Pension's regulatory permissions not addressed | Insurance Distribution | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: High**

The arrangement places insurance in front of employers inside Smart Pension's platform, with Smart remunerated by a volume-linked share of Kota's commission. Whether that makes Smart an **introducer** or an **insurance distributor in its own right** was not raised. The volume-linked remuneration in **F2** cuts against a pure unremunerated-introducer analysis. **The counterparty raised this itself five days later — see F19.**

- **Reference:** IDD Art.2(1)(1), Art.3; FCA PERG 5 and ICOBS *(confirm)*; S.I. No. 229/2018 registration requirements *(confirm exact regulation)*.

#### F6 — Success criteria are sales-volume only | Conduct | PC
**Priority: Medium · Impact: Medium**

Contractual success criteria are gross written premium and attachment rate, with an explicit walk-away trigger: "if we haven't hit that, then it's like, that's complete grounds. Walk away here" ([30:05](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=1805)). No customer-outcome measure — claims experience, complaints, lapse or cancellation rates, evidence that cover suited the employers who bought it — forms any part of the criteria.

- **Reference:** CPC 2025 Standards for Business — Core Standard; CBI Guidance on Securing Customers' Interests; FCA Consumer Duty PRIN 2A.2 and outcomes monitoring *(confirm)*; Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/2358 Art.7 and Art.10.

#### F7 — NDA coverage for commercially sensitive exchange not confirmed on call | Governance | C (observation)
**Priority: Low · Impact: Low**

Both sides exchanged confidential commercial data — Smart's book economics and its £4m by 2030 cross-sell target ([11:08](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=668)), Kota's commission rate and conversion assumptions. No issue alleged; confirm the executed NDA covers the pilot workstream.

#### F8 — Key-person dependency over the handover period | Governance | C (observation)
**Priority: Low · Impact: Low**

Kota's lead was going on two weeks' planned leave, with a handover to Luke prepared the previous day ([05:32](https://app.fireflies.ai/view/01KTRC5GZGKXS354TDMMQRA6SA?t=332)). Not a compliance issue, and no comment is made on the leave itself. Noted only because the regulatory pre-conditions in F2–F5 must appear on the handover alongside the commercial actions.

## Call — 11 June 2026 — John / Rachna Regroup

| Field | Detail |

| **Recording consent requested** | **No** |

### Purpose

Understanding the counterparty's global benefits strategy following a four-way merger, and positioning Kota as its benefits infrastructure — starting with an EMEA IPMI plan and a referral-fee commercial model. The counterparty currently sources cover market-by-market through local brokers, with SafetyWings used for international plans in APAC.

### What was done well

- **Genuinely good process discovery.** John walked the incumbent process end to end — how a request arrives, how quotes are sourced, who signs off, who enrols, who owns renewals, whether the HR advisors are the counterparty's own staff.
- **Offered an aggregate alternative to employee-level data**, and narrowed the ask when pressed: *"if you can't pull this data, then... just this market data, so like how many employees let's say in Spain"* and later *"it's gender age... where their location, they're the main things"*.
- **Raised an NDA unprompted** — *"we're happy to sign NDA's if need be to do this"*.
- **Did not over-promise on migrating existing insured members** — *"it depends on the plan... it depends on the contract like you'd have to kind of guide us on that"*, and said he would bring the benefits team into the follow-up.
- Identified that anti-selection rules bear on introducing a new plan.

### Findings

#### F9 — Legal conclusion asserted about the counterparty's existing practice | Conduct / Competence | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: Medium**

*"they're putting 10, I say 10, 20% on top of the premium and taking that... ultimately that is illegal. I just be direct and honest, it's called price manipulation, because these are insurance products, you're not allowed to do that with insurance products, it's not like a normal consumer product, it's not a re-selling product."*

Whether or not the underlying point is directionally right, a commercial lead should not be delivering a legal conclusion on the lawfulness of a third party's pricing to that third party. It is outside competence, unverified, and used as a sales argument against the incumbent arrangement.

- **Reference:** CPC 2025 Standards for Business — competence; [[Minimum Competency Code]] where the statement touches the regulated product; IDD Art.17(1)–(2).

#### F10 — Fee-stacking model explained without a fair value or disclosure position | Conduct / Product Governance | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: High**

Immediately after calling premium mark-up illegal, John set out the model other partners use and quantified the return: a management fee of *"anywhere between 15 to 25 euro per month... per employee per month"*, producing *"300 euro per annum just by having the benefits"*, **on top of** the €50 per policy per year referral fee out of Kota's own 10–20% commission.

Three layers of cost — insurer premium, Kota commission, partner referral fee, plus a partner management fee borne by the employer or employee. The distinction between an unlawful premium mark-up and a lawful service fee turns entirely on whether the fee buys genuine services and is disclosed. Neither point was addressed.

- **Reference:** S.I. No. 229/2018 Reg.23(1)(h) and IDD Art.19(1)(d)–(e); CPC 2025 Parts 3 and 4; FCA Consumer Duty PRIN 2A.4 *(confirm)*; Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/2358 Art.7 and Art.10; value-for-money direction in [[EIOPA Peer Review on Product Oversight and Governance — 2023]] and the [[EU Retail Investment Strategy — Product Governance Changes (Dec 2025)]] "undue costs" concept.

#### F11 — Counterparty's regulatory perimeter status not addressed | Insurance Distribution | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: High**

The counterparty's own HR advisors would present plans, enrol members, add dependants and cancel policies — *"they're going to send directly to that specific broker or specific provider... we have so many enrollments for this month or we have X, Y, Z who are [lapsed]. So we are going to cancel the policy"* — while the counterparty earns a per-policy referral fee and a per-employee management fee. Introducer, ancillary insurance intermediary, or distributor requiring registration? Never tested.

- **Reference:** IDD Art.2(1)(1), Art.2(1)(4) and Art.1(3) (ancillary exemption and its conditions), Art.3; S.I. No. 229/2018 *(confirm)*; FCA PERG 5 *(confirm)*.

#### F12 — Named partner's commercial data disclosed to a prospect | Confidentiality | NC
**Priority: High · Impact: Medium**

*"We have like a European international plan for the likes of remote. There's about 10,000 on the plan... you can see how they're booking close to four million in this one plan for the Europe alone."* Deel and Remote were named and their internal commentary relayed — *"they came to us and said... we wish you found you two years ago"*.

Client-confidential commercial information given to a third party with no NDA in place — John confirmed on the same call that an NDA had not yet been signed.

- **Why NC:** the disclosure has already occurred and cannot be remediated by a control change. Repeat of a Q1 2026 finding of the same type (09/03/2026; and 30/04/2026, where the same three partner relationships were disclosed). Third occurrence in two quarters, now involving revenue figures. A fourth, milder instance appears on the Justworks call — see **F36 context**.

#### F13 — Employee-level personal data requested before an NDA or data processing terms were in place | Data Protection | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: Medium**

Census data requested at member level — *"the member reference, the status, date of birth, age, gender, nationality, country of work"* — to shop the risk to carriers (Bupa, Vitality, AXA named). No NDA and no data processing agreement existed; the counterparty said she would check the NDA position before providing the second dataset.

- **Reference:** GDPR Art.5(1)(c), Art.6, Art.13–14, Art.28, and Art.44 et seq. where LatAm or APAC lives are in scope.
- Nationality is not an Art.9 special category but is a proxy for racial or ethnic origin, and health-insurance census data can imply health status.

#### F14 — Kota described as "the broker" in non-UK markets | Consumer Protection / Regulatory Status | PC
**Priority: Medium · Impact: Medium**

*"one broker, aka tech platform, that is ourselves"*, *"we are the broker"*, in a conversation expressly about Portugal, Spain, France, LatAm and APAC. Regulatory status asserted only as *"we are financially regulated"*, without identifying the regulator or the scope of the permission.

- **Reference:** S.I. No. 229/2018 Reg.23(1)(a)–(d); IDD Art.18(a)(i).
- Direct repeat of the 09/03/2026 Q1 finding — *"He said we're a broker, but we can only describe ourselves as that in the UK"* — and of 12/01/2026 against a different individual. Recurs again on the Justworks call, **F37**.

## Call — 15 June 2026 — Contractual Terms, Kota / Smart Pension

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Date of call** | 15 June 2026 |
| **Duration** | ~30 minutes (no audible content for the first 2½ minutes) |
| **Kota attendee** | John Higgins |
| **Counterparty** | Smart Pension — Rosie Crowley |
| **Call type** | Embed partner — contractual and commercial terms |
| **Consumer present** | No |
| **Recording consent requested** | **No** |
| **Overall rating** | **PC** |

### Purpose

Walking through two commercial proposals for the Smart Pension partnership and agreeing what goes into the contract: a 12-month pilot followed by 24 months (three-year term with a one-year opt-out), a GWP success trigger, revenue share, a platform licence fee, exclusivity, marketing commitments, and the advice/guidance workflow.

### What was done well

- **Did not commit to exclusivity on the spot.** *"I need to chat with Luke... I would need to take that away"* — correct escalation instinct on a term with material consequences.
- **Was transparent that no competing master trust relationship exists**, and framed exclusivity as needing commercial compensation rather than refusing outright.
- Offered both a short-form order form and the full MSA route, and explained honestly why the short form exists — *"it would take ages to get the deal over the line because there's a lot in that MSA"*.
- **The counterparty asked the right question.** Rosie raised the appointed-representative point unprompted. That the question was asked is to her credit; that it went unanswered is **F19**.

### Findings

#### F18 — Volume-banded rate card plus a fixed licence fee, still with no conflicts or fair value assessment | Conduct / Insurance Distribution | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: High**

What was a sliding scale in principle on 10 June is now a contractual rate card. Two options were presented: a flat 50/50 revenue share with a platform licence fee, or a **GWP-banded sliding scale** — *"up to 2 million gross written premium is 70% Kota and 30% Smart and you can kind of see from there then how it incrementally changes."* John's own summary: ***"it's rewarding for volume."***

Layered on top is a fixed licence fee payable by the partner — the transcript is unreliable on the amount (see quality caveat), but the counterparty's own reading was *"150k per annum"*. A **fixed fee the partner must cover, combined with commission share that rises with volume**, compounds the conflict in **F2**: the partner now has a floor to earn back as well as an uplift to chase.

Note the counterparty's position on 10 June was that members would not bear a platform fee — *"we wouldn't charge our employees in that way"*. The fee here falls on Smart, not members, which is the right side of that line. The conflict is not about who pays the fee; it is about what the fee plus the volume banding incentivises.

- **Reference:** IDD Art.17(3), Art.24; Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/2358 Art.8 and Art.10; FCA ICOBS 2.3 and SYSC 10 *(confirm)*.
- **Action:** the assessment in **A2** must now be completed against the actual rate card, not the concept, and before signature. Figures to be confirmed from audio.

#### F19 — The counterparty asked whether it needed to be an appointed representative, and the question was not answered | Insurance Distribution | PC
**Priority: High · Impact: High**

Rosie: *"we are, we have a ability to do this from a legal perspective, because you, you're pointing us a rep. Is that the sort of…?"* — read as *"you're appointing us a rep"*, i.e. appointed representative status.

John's answer described the operational guidance workflow instead: *"how that works is obviously, Smart customer goes in. It's like, oh, great. I can buy a group scheme now and I'll look at Bupa, Vitality..."* — going on to explain how queries get routed. He also acknowledged the partner's status in passing: *"because we're not a [CBI] regulated"* — read as Smart not being regulated.

So the perimeter question is now **on the record, asked by the party whose permissions are in question, and unanswered.** This converts **F5** and **F11** from an omission into a live unresolved question that a counterparty is relying on Kota to have settled.

- **Reference:** FCA PERG 5; appointed representative regime under FSMA s.39 and SUP 12 *(confirm)*; IDD Art.3.

### The structural point for the management report

Q1's remediation was aimed at customer-facing sales calls — a formal script for individuals not MCC-qualified, induction slides on Skillcast, and a rule that one named individual should not take calls alone. **Those controls worked.** Across five calls and roughly three hours of partner conversation, not one instance of advice to a consumer, and on the Justworks call the Embed lead three times refused to answer a product question he was unsure of.

The same failure modes reappeared one layer up, in the **design of the distribution arrangements themselves** — where no script applies, no compliance gate exists, and the participants are senior staff who were not the subject of the Q1 remediation. Two insurance distribution arrangements were specified across June in commercial, contractual and technical detail. Compliance, Legal, the DPO and product governance appear in none of the five conversations. The counterparty asked the regulatory question that Kota should have raised, and did not get an answer.

The fix is to extend the control sideways and upstream, not to tighten it further on sales.

## Programme-Level Finding

#### G1 — The monitoring population is not reliably reachable by Compliance | Governance / Monitoring | NC
**Priority: High · Impact: High**

Full coverage was achieved this quarter, but not by any repeatable route. One recording was reachable because the meeting owner had uploaded it to Fireflies; one because a transcript was handed over directly; three because the audio was transcribed locally after the fact. The underlying position is unchanged: Embed partner recordings are saved as raw audio to Drive under the meeting owner's account, reach Fireflies only if that person uploads them, and default to owner-only privacy when they do.

Monitoring that depends on individual uploads or manual hand-off is not a control. And the quarter settles the question of whether it matters: five calls produced **42 findings including one NC**, most of them structural and several of them go-live blockers. An unreachable population cannot be assumed clean.

**Recommended remediation:**
1. Put the notetaker on **all** Embed partner calls automatically via the calendar integration, rather than relying on manual upload.
2. Set transcript privacy for partner calls so Compliance has standing access, or add Compliance to a dedicated Fireflies channel for Embed calls.
3. Where audio-only recordings are retained in Drive, store them where Compliance can retrieve them; note that files above 10 MB cannot be pulled programmatically, which is why three of these needed local transcription.
4. Note that **F26** puts some partnership discussion on WhatsApp, which no monitoring arrangement will capture.
