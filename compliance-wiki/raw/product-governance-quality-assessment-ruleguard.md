# How Good Is Your Product Governance?

*Adapted from Ruleguard — "How Good Is Your Product Governance Framework?" Original content focused broadly on financial services; adapted here for insurance distribution under IDD/IDR and CPC 2025.*

**Source:** https://www.ruleguard.com/blog/post/howgoodisyourproductgovernance

---

## The Challenge

Product governance is increasingly a regulatory priority. More than 60% of firms admit they struggle to keep track of evolving regulations and maintain full visibility over product performance. Common challenges include:

- **Inconsistent oversight** across different products and distribution channels
- **Manual processes** that don't scale and create gaps in monitoring
- **Difficulty defining target markets** accurately, particularly for products distributed through multiple channels
- **Lack of management information (MI)** to demonstrate effective governance to regulators
- **No clear audit trail** of product decisions, assessments, and reviews

For insurance intermediaries, these challenges are compounded by the requirement to manage governance across multiple insurer relationships, product types, and distribution channels simultaneously.

## What Good Looks Like

An effective product governance framework for insurance distribution includes:

### 1. Clear Product Strategy

- Each product has a documented **target market definition** (positive and negative)
- The **distribution strategy** is explicitly aligned with the target market
- There is a clear **product approval process** with defined roles and sign-off authority
- The strategy covers the full product lifecycle — design, distribution, monitoring, and retirement

### 2. Robust Target Market Assessment

- Target markets defined using structured criteria:
  - Customer type (employer size, sector, jurisdiction)
  - Knowledge and experience (familiarity with insurance products)
  - Financial situation (ability to sustain premiums)
  - Risk tolerance (appetite for excesses, waiting periods, exclusions)
  - Objectives and needs (employee retention, compliance, wellbeing)
- **Negative target market** clearly documented — who the product is not for
- Target market definitions reviewed at least annually and on trigger events

### 3. Effective Distribution Oversight

- Distribution channels assessed for compatibility with the target market
- Where products are distributed through partners or third-party platforms, the intermediary must satisfy itself that the channel reaches the intended customers
- Partner arrangements include provisions for product governance information sharing
- Evidence that products are **not** reaching customers outside the target market

### 4. Ongoing Monitoring and MI

- **Annual product reviews** as a minimum, covering:
  - Sales volumes and distribution channel breakdown
  - Complaints data (volume, nature, trends)
  - Claims experience and loss ratios
  - Lapse rates and cancellation patterns
  - Evidence of products reaching unintended customers
- **Review triggers** defined and documented:
  - Spike in complaints
  - Significant claims deterioration
  - Regulatory change
  - Market event affecting the target market
  - Change in distribution channel or partner arrangement
- **Management information** reported to senior management and the board

### 5. Clear Audit Trail

- All product approval decisions documented with rationale
- Target market assessments and reviews recorded
- Distribution channel assessments evidenced
- Complaint and claims analysis retained
- Correspondence with manufacturers about product performance and target market feedback

## Key Takeaway

Good product governance requires a clear strategy, structured processes, ongoing monitoring, and a robust audit trail. Firms that rely on manual or ad-hoc processes risk both regulatory action and customer harm. The framework should be proportionate to the firm's size and product range, but must cover the full lifecycle from design to retirement.
