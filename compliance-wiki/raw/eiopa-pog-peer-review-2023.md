# EIOPA Peer Review on Product Oversight and Governance (POG) — 2023

*EIOPA's cross-EU peer review assessing how national competent authorities (NCAs) supervise insurance product governance. Findings are directly relevant to how insurance firms should expect their POG arrangements to be supervised.*

**Source:** https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/publications/peer-review-product-oversight-and-governance-pog_en

---

## Overview

Published 20 July 2023, this peer review covered the period from the application of the Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD) on 1 October 2018 to 31 March 2022. All 30 national competent authorities participated.

The review focused primarily on insurance-based investment products (IBIPs) but its findings on governance frameworks, target market practices, and supervisory expectations are applicable to all insurance products.

## Key Findings

### 1. Significant Variation in POG Supervision Across the EU

NCAs showed significant differences in their level of POG supervision. Some NCAs had mature, proactive supervisory approaches; others had done minimal POG-specific work beyond checking that arrangements formally existed.

**Implication for distributors:** The quality of POG supervision you receive depends on your jurisdiction. Firms operating across multiple EU markets (as Kota does) should apply the highest standard consistently rather than relying on local supervisory expectations.

### 2. Principle-Based Regulation Creates Ambiguity

Several NCAs reported challenges supervising POG requirements due to the **principle-based nature** of Delegated Regulation 2017/2358. Without detailed prescriptive rules, supervisors could not go beyond checking whether arrangements were formally in place — they struggled to assess whether they were **effective**.

**Implication for distributors:** Firms should not rely on the principle-based nature of the regulation to apply a minimal approach. Regulators are moving toward more prescriptive expectations, and firms with weak frameworks will be exposed.

### 3. Target Market Definitions Often Too Broad

The review found instances where manufacturers defined target markets so broadly that they provided **no meaningful guidance** to distributors. A target market of "all retail customers" is effectively no target market at all.

**Implication for distributors:** Distributors should push back on manufacturers who provide overly broad target market definitions. If the manufacturer's target market definition is too broad, the distributor must **refine it** based on its own customer knowledge.

### 4. Product Testing Inconsistently Applied

Product testing — including scenario analysis as required by Article 6 of Delegated Regulation 2017/2358 — was inconsistently applied. Some manufacturers performed robust testing; others treated it as a tick-box exercise.

**Implication for distributors:** Distributors should request evidence of product testing from manufacturers. If testing has not been adequately performed, this is a risk that should be escalated.

### 5. Value for Money Emerging as Key Focus

The review highlighted the growing importance of **value for money** assessments. EIOPA signalled that POG supervision will increasingly focus on whether products deliver fair value — not just whether governance processes exist.

**Implication for distributors:** Expect value-for-money assessments to become a standard part of product governance reviews. Distributors should be prepared to demonstrate that products distributed offer fair value relative to their costs.

### 6. Distributor Feedback Loop Often Weak

The review found that the feedback loop from distributors to manufacturers was often weak or non-existent. Distributors were not proactively sharing sales data, complaints, or market intelligence with manufacturers.

**Implication for distributors:** This is a regulatory obligation under Article 10 of Delegated Regulation 2017/2358. Distributors must establish processes for sharing relevant information with manufacturers, including complaints data, sales volumes, and evidence of products reaching unintended customers.

## EIOPA Recommendations

EIOPA issued **110 recommendations** to NCAs, covering:

1. **Organisation and resources** — NCAs should dedicate adequate resources to POG supervision
2. **Risk-based supervision** — NCAs should integrate POG into their risk-based supervisory frameworks
3. **Supervisory methodologies** — NCAs should develop specific tools for assessing the effectiveness (not just existence) of POG arrangements
4. **Target market** — NCAs should assess whether target markets are defined at a sufficiently granular level
5. **Product testing** — NCAs should verify that product testing includes genuine scenario analysis
6. **Value for money** — NCAs should assess whether products deliver fair value to customers
7. **Monitoring and review** — NCAs should assess the quality of ongoing product monitoring

## 2026 Outlook

EIOPA's 2026 work programme signals:
- POG supervision will extend **beyond IBIPs** to all insurance products (including non-life and group products)
- **Value for money** will be a central focus
- Enhanced **conduct supervision** addressing issues beyond product design
