# Product Governance Requirements — Manufacturer and Distributor Obligations

*Adapted from KPMG Ireland — "MiFID II Product Governance Requirements." Original content focused on MiFID II investment product governance; adapted here for insurance distribution under IDD/IDR (S.I. No. 229/2018) and the Revised Consumer Protection Code 2025. The manufacturer/distributor framework, target market obligations, and oversight requirements apply equally to insurance product governance.*

**Source:** https://kpmg.com/ie/en/insights/consulting/mifid-ii-product-governance-requirements.html

---

## Overview

Insurance manufacturers (insurers) and distributors (intermediaries) must put in place robust processes for:

1. The **design** of insurance products
2. The **identification of a target market** — customers for whom the product is compatible, and those for whom it is not
3. The **ongoing monitoring** of distribution activities to ensure products reach the intended target market

These obligations are set out in the Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD), Art.25, transposed in Ireland by S.I. No. 229/2018, Reg.38, and reinforced by the CBI's Consumer Protection Code 2025 and the FCA's Consumer Duty (PRIN 2A.3).

## Manufacturer Obligations

### Product Approval Process

Manufacturers must have a formal **product approval process** that is completed before any product is marketed or distributed to customers. This process must:

- Be **proportionate** to the nature of the insurance product
- Identify the **target market** for each product
- Assess all **relevant risks** to the target market
- Ensure the **distribution strategy is consistent** with the target market
- Be **documented** and subject to regular review

### Target Market Definition

The manufacturer must define:

- **Positive target market** — the group of customers for whom the product is designed and compatible
- **Negative target market** — customers for whom the product is clearly **not** compatible
- **Distribution channels** through which the product is expected to be sold

Target market definitions should be sufficiently granular to avoid a situation where the target market is so broad that it provides no meaningful guidance to distributors.

### Five Target Market Categories

Adapted from the ESMA framework, manufacturers should assess the target market across five categories:

| Category | Insurance Application |
|----------|----------------------|
| **Customer type** | Employer size, industry sector, group size, jurisdiction, employee demographics |
| **Knowledge & experience** | HR/benefits team familiarity with group insurance, claims processes, benefits administration |
| **Financial situation** | Employer's ability to sustain premiums, co-insurance capacity, financial stability |
| **Risk tolerance** | Appetite for waiting periods, excesses, exclusions, benefit caps |
| **Objectives & needs** | Employee retention, regulatory compliance, wellbeing strategy, tax efficiency |

### Information to Distributors

Manufacturers must make available to distributors:

- The target market definition (positive and negative)
- Details of the product approval process
- Appropriate product information enabling the distributor to understand the product and recommend it appropriately

### Periodic Review

Manufacturers must regularly review each product, considering:

- Whether the product continues to meet the needs of the target market
- Whether the distribution strategy remains appropriate
- Any events that could materially affect the potential risk to the target market
- Complaints and claims data
- Changes in regulation or market conditions

## Distributor Obligations

### Understanding the Product

Distributors must obtain from the manufacturer the information necessary to:

- **Understand the product** — features, risks, costs, target market
- **Understand the intended target market** — who it is designed for and who it is not
- Assess whether the product is **compatible with the distributor's own customer base**

### Product Distribution Arrangements

Distributors must have adequate arrangements in place to ensure:

- Products are **distributed to the identified target market**
- Products are **not** distributed to customers in the negative target market
- Distribution channels are **appropriate** for the product and its target market

### Refining the Target Market

Where a distributor has a more detailed understanding of its customer base than the manufacturer, it should **refine the target market** at a more granular level. For example:

- A manufacturer may define the target market as "SME employers with 10–250 employees"
- A distributor operating exclusively in the technology sector may refine this to "technology SMEs with 10–250 employees and existing benefits programmes"

### Regular Review

Distributors must periodically review:

- Whether products remain **consistent with the needs** of the identified target market
- Whether products are **actually reaching** the intended target market
- Whether any products are being sold to customers **outside** the target market

### Feedback to Manufacturers

Distributors have a proactive obligation to share relevant information with manufacturers, including:

- **Sales and distribution data** — volumes, channel mix, customer demographics
- **Complaints data** — volume, nature, trends related to the product
- **Market intelligence** — changes in customer needs, competitive developments
- **Any evidence** that a product is not meeting the needs of the target market or is reaching unintended customers

This feedback loop is essential for the manufacturer's periodic review to be effective.

## Oversight of Distribution

Manufacturers must oversee distributors to ensure compliance with the intended distribution strategy. This includes:

- Assessing whether distributors **understand the product and target market**
- Monitoring whether the distribution strategy is being **followed in practice**
- Taking action where products are being **mis-sold** or reaching unintended customers
- Considering whether to **restrict or terminate** distribution arrangements where governance concerns arise

## Record-Keeping

Both manufacturers and distributors must maintain:

- Records of **product approval decisions** and rationale
- **Target market definitions** (positive and negative)
- Evidence of **periodic reviews** and outcomes
- **Distribution data** and channel assessments
- **Correspondence** between manufacturer and distributor regarding product governance
- Records retained for at least **5 years** after the end of the product's lifecycle

## Key Takeaway

Product governance is a shared responsibility. Manufacturers define the target market and distribution strategy; distributors must understand, apply, refine, and report back. Neither party can delegate its obligations to the other. The framework requires structured processes, documented decisions, regular review, and a genuine feedback loop between manufacturer and distributor.
