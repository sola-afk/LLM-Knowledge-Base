# Product Governance Guidelines — Target Market Assessment and Oversight

*Adapted from ESMA Guidelines on MiFID II Product Governance Requirements (A&L Goodbody briefing). Original content focused on MiFID II investment products; adapted here for insurance distribution under IDD/IDR and CPC 2025. The core principles — target market identification, manufacturer/distributor responsibilities, periodic review — apply equally to insurance product governance.*

**Source:** https://www.algoodbody.com/files/uploads/news_insights_pub/ESMA_Guidelines_on_MiFID_II_v2.pdf

## Overview

Product governance requirements are a key element of the customer protection framework. They ensure that insurance products are manufactured and distributed in the best interests of the customer. Firms manufacturing products for sale, or distributing products to customers, must meet product governance obligations including:

- Identification and periodic review of the **target market** for each product
- Development of a **distribution strategy** consistent with the target market
- Regular **product reviews** to assess whether products continue to deliver fair outcomes

## Manufacturer and Distributor Definitions

**Manufacturer:** An insurance undertaking or intermediary that creates, develops, designs, or significantly adapts an insurance product. Co-manufacturing applies where multiple firms collaborate on product design.

**Distributor:** An insurance intermediary or other firm that offers, recommends, or sells insurance products to customers. Distributors do not design the product but must understand it and ensure it reaches the appropriate target market.

*Under the IDD (Art.25) and S.I. No. 229/2018 (Reg.38), the same manufacturer/distributor framework applies as under MiFID II, tailored for insurance.*

## Five Target Market Assessment Categories

When assessing the target market, both manufacturers and distributors should consider five categories. These originate from ESMA guidance but are directly applicable to insurance product governance:

### 1. Customer Type
- **Insurance context:** Employer type (SME, mid-market, enterprise), industry sector, group size, jurisdiction
- Is this product designed for specific employer segments or broadly available?

### 2. Knowledge and Experience
- **Insurance context:** The employer's or HR team's familiarity with group insurance products, benefits administration, and claims processes
- Does the product require a certain level of understanding to be used effectively?

### 3. Financial Situation
- **Insurance context:** The employer's ability to sustain premium commitments, including any risk-sharing arrangements (e.g., co-insurance, excesses)
- Could the premium structure cause financial difficulty for certain employer types?

### 4. Risk Tolerance
- **Insurance context:** Appetite for product features that involve trade-offs — waiting periods, coverage exclusions, benefit caps, claims thresholds
- Products with longer waiting periods or higher excesses suit customers with higher risk tolerance

### 5. Objectives and Needs
- **Insurance context:** What the employer is trying to achieve — employee retention, regulatory compliance (e.g., auto-enrolment), employee wellbeing, tax efficiency
- Does the product align with these objectives?

## Negative Target Market

In addition to defining who the product is designed **for**, firms must identify who it is **not** designed for:

- Employers whose employees would be systematically excluded by underwriting criteria
- Employers in jurisdictions where the product cannot legally be distributed
- Employers whose financial situation makes the premium structure unsustainable
- Customers whose needs clearly don't match the product's features

## Manufacturer Obligations

1. **Product approval process** — maintain, operate, and review before any product is marketed
2. **Target market identification** — define positive and negative target markets using the five categories
3. **Risk assessment** — assess all relevant risks to the identified target market
4. **Distribution strategy** — design a distribution strategy consistent with the target market; select distribution channels accordingly
5. **Information to distributors** — provide distributors with all appropriate information on the product, the approval process, and the target market definition
6. **Periodic review** — regularly review products considering events that could materially affect risk to the target market
7. **Oversee distributors** — ensure distributors and sub-distributors comply with the manufacturer's distribution strategy

## Distributor Obligations

1. **Understand the product** — obtain from manufacturers the information needed to understand the product and its intended target market
2. **Product distribution arrangements** — have adequate arrangements to ensure products reach the identified target market
3. **Refine the target market** — where appropriate, define the target market at a more granular level based on the distributor's knowledge of its customer base
4. **Regular review** — periodically review whether products remain consistent with the needs of the identified target market
5. **Feedback to manufacturers** — proactively inform the manufacturer of relevant information that could support product reviews (e.g., complaints data, distribution outcomes, customer feedback)
6. **Record-keeping** — maintain records of target market assessments, distribution decisions, and review outcomes

## Periodic Review Requirements

- **Frequency:** At minimum annually; more frequently for complex or high-risk products
- **Depth:** Proportionate to the nature of the product — simpler products may need lighter review
- **Scope of review:**
  - Is the product still meeting the needs of the target market?
  - Is the distribution strategy still appropriate?
  - Are there complaints or claims patterns suggesting customer detriment?
  - Have there been regulatory changes affecting the product?
  - Is the product reaching customers outside the intended target market?
- **Trigger events:** Any material change should prompt an immediate ad-hoc review outside the regular cycle

## Key Takeaway

Product governance is a shared responsibility between manufacturers and distributors. The five target market categories provide a structured framework for assessing product suitability. Distributors cannot simply rely on the manufacturer's target market definition — they must apply it to their own customer base and distribution channels, refine it where necessary, and feed information back to the manufacturer.
