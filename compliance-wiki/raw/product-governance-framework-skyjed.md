## What Is a Product Governance Framework?

A product governance framework is a comprehensive framework of policies, processes, and technologies that enables insurance firms to manage products throughout their entire lifecycle — from design to retirement — while ensuring regulatory compliance, maximising customer value, and minimising risk.

For insurance intermediaries and distributors, product governance is not optional. The Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD), transposed in Ireland via S.I. No. 229/2018, Regulation 38, requires both manufacturers (insurers) and distributors (intermediaries) to have formal product oversight and governance arrangements in place.

## The Three Phases of Product Governance

### Phase 1: Design

The design phase covers product ideation, development, and approval. For **insurance manufacturers** (insurers), this means:

- Defining the **target market** — who is the product designed for?
- Conducting a **risk assessment** for the target market
- Designing a **distribution strategy** consistent with the target market
- Running a **product approval process** before any product is marketed or distributed

For **insurance distributors** (intermediaries), the design phase is about understanding the manufacturer's intent:

- Obtaining the manufacturer's **target market definition**
- Understanding the product's **intended distribution channels**
- Assessing whether the product fits the distributor's **customer base and distribution model**

**Regulatory basis:** S.I. No. 229/2018, Reg.38(1)–(4) (manufacturers); Reg.38(6)–(8) (distributors); IDD, Art.25(1)–(3)

### Phase 2: Distribution

The distribution phase ensures products reach the right customers through appropriate channels. Key obligations:

- **Target market alignment** — products must only be distributed to customers within the identified target market
- **Negative target market** — identify customers for whom the product is **not** appropriate
- **Distribution channel assessment** — ensure the channel (e.g., direct platform, API-driven partner integration, broker) is consistent with the product's target market
- **Information provision** — provide customers with clear, accurate, timely product information including IPIDs for non-life products

Distributors must have adequate **product distribution arrangements** to ensure products reach the identified target market and are not sold to customers outside it.

**Regulatory basis:** S.I. No. 229/2018, Reg.38(7); IDD, Art.25(3); CPC 2025, Part 3 (information provision); FCA Consumer Duty, PRIN 2A.3

### Phase 3: Monitoring, Review, and Improvement

Once a product is live, firms need ongoing oversight:

- **Ongoing monitoring** of whether products continue to meet the needs of the target market
- **Periodic reviews** — at minimum annually, assessing product performance, complaints data, and distribution outcomes
- **Review triggers** — events that should prompt an immediate review outside the regular cycle:
  - Significant change in the product's risk profile
  - Material change in the target market
  - Significant increase in complaints related to the product
  - Regulatory change affecting the product
  - Material change in the distribution channel or partner arrangement
  - Evidence the product is reaching customers outside the target market
- **Product retirement** — where a product no longer meets the target market's needs or poses unacceptable risk, firms should have a process for withdrawing or modifying it

**Regulatory basis:** S.I. No. 229/2018, Reg.38(3), (8); IDD, Art.25(1), (4); CPC 2025, Standards for Business (ongoing governance obligations)

## Building a Product Governance Framework

### Step 1: Define Policies and Procedures

Document a clear product governance policy covering:
- Roles and responsibilities (who designs, approves, distributes, monitors)
- Product approval process and sign-off authority
- Target market definition methodology
- Review cycle and trigger criteria
- Escalation and remediation processes

### Step 2: Establish Target Market Definitions

For each insurance product, document:
- **Positive target market** — customers for whom the product is designed
- **Negative target market** — customers for whom the product is not suitable
- **Distribution channel alignment** — which channels are appropriate

Adapt the five target market assessment categories (originally from ESMA/MiFID II, equally applicable to insurance distribution):
1. **Customer type** — employer size, industry sector, employee demographics
2. **Knowledge and experience** — familiarity with insurance products
3. **Financial situation** — ability to sustain premium commitments
4. **Risk tolerance** — appetite for product features like excesses, waiting periods, exclusions
5. **Objectives and needs** — what the customer is trying to achieve (employee retention, regulatory compliance, employee wellbeing)

### Step 3: Implement Monitoring and Reporting

- Schedule annual product reviews as a minimum
- Define management information (MI) requirements — what data is needed to assess whether products are reaching the target market
- Establish KPIs: complaints rates, claims ratios, lapse rates, distribution channel mix
- Create a reporting line to senior management and the board

### Step 4: Document and Maintain an Audit Trail

- Record all product approval decisions and rationale
- Maintain evidence of target market assessments
- Log all periodic reviews and trigger-based reviews
- Keep records for at least 5 years (aligned with CJA 2010, s.55 record-keeping requirements)

## Key Takeaway

Product governance is not a one-off compliance exercise — it is a continuous lifecycle process. The framework must be embedded in how products are designed, distributed, and monitored, with clear ownership, regular review, and documented decision-making at every stage.
