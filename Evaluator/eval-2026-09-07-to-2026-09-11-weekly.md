### HF-05 confirmed — the AE contribution base, stated wrong

> **Simon Ward, 23:37**: *"that 1.5% on the my future fund is taken from their **net pay**, whereas
> that 2% is taken from their gross profit."*

`source-of-truth-ae-myfuturefund.md` §5, verbatim gov.ie:

> *"Contributions are calculated on your **gross earnings**, so anything included in the gross pay
> field of a payroll file will be assessable. Contributions will not, however, be levied on any
> gross pay over €80,000."*

My Future Fund contributions come from **gross earnings**, not net pay. This is a direct
contradiction of the source-of-truth, so **HF-05 confirmed** under R3 — a specific fact stated to
the customer that contradicts gov.ie.

### This is a repeat, and it has got worse

### HF-04 — arranging, by someone with no authorisation to arrange

This is the core of it. Mark is not on the register, and the call's entire purpose is to arrange
regulated products: he solicits a **broker-of-record form**, offers to **go to market for group
life, critical illness and income protection**, and proposes a follow-up call to walk the customer
through the options.

MCC 2017 limb (b) — *arranging or offering to arrange* a retail financial product — is a regulated
activity. IDR S.I. 229/2018 Reg. 5 and FSMA s. 19 exist to prevent exactly this. Q2-25-F-01 was
graded Fail for a narrower version (offering to request a quote).

### HF-09 — "there's no cost to you", and the customer caught it

> **Mark, 09:14**: *"We don't charge any broker fees because the way brokerage works, we get paid
> commission by your health insurance policy being set up or group life ... by the provider."*
>
> **Mark, 10:02**: *"we get the commission from that and **there's no cost to you involved** in that
> sort of things."*
>
> **Sophia (customer), 09:36**: *"it's not baked into the premiums that we pay like your
> commission?"*
>
> **Mark, 09:40**: *"The commission would be built into ... you pay the 100% price and then they
> give a percentage of it is the way it works."*

He led with *"no cost to you"*, and only corrected to *"built into ... the 100% price"* **because
the customer challenged him**. Those two statements cannot both be true. Commission funded from
the premium is a cost to the customer.

No schedule of fees and remuneration was offered, which IDR Reg. 23(1)(h) and IDD Art. 19(1)(d)–(e)
require. He did volunteer an indicative range — *"it could be between 3 and 6% depending on
providers"* — which is more disclosure than the audit usually sees, and is genuinely to his credit.

### HF-03 — "go to the market", and the customer had to find the limit on the website

> **Sophia, 14:15**: *"When I was on your website earlier, it mentioned that you **don't have access
> to the whole market**. Does that mean that you exclusively work with certain insurers or are you
> able to do a full, like review of all the different insurances available to us on the market?"*
>
> **Mark, 14:36**: *"we work with Unum Metlife ... There's three or four providers within that."*

Asked directly, he answered **honestly and immediately** — and that matters. But by that point he
had used *"go to the market"* or *"go to market"* repeatedly, and he continued afterwards:
*"We don't have preferential providers we work with"*, *"we don't steer you in any direction"*.

The disclosure happened because the customer had read the website and pressed. `req-detection-
criteria.md` names this exact pattern under HF-03: *"we go to the market and get the most
competitive quotes"* when the panel is limited. **Q1-26-SF-01 was graded Severe Fail on this
basis.**

### The Grade 5 argument, flagged not decided

Grade 5 is *"customer misled on firm capability, scope, or a material fact"*. Two candidates: the
*"no cost to you"* commission statement, and the *"go to the market"* framing against a three-to-
four provider panel. Q1-26-SF-01 set a Severe Fail precedent for the latter.

Graded **4** because on both points he **corrected himself accurately when asked** — he was
imprecise rather than concealing, and named the providers without hesitation. Compliance should
decide whether that distinction holds, particularly given the corrections came only after customer
challenge.

### Why this is a systemic finding, not a coaching one

Mark has been at Kota **two months** and is running regulated-product sales calls solo. He told the
customer he has *"about 10 years in employee benefits before this"* — plausible competence, but MCC
authorisation is not transferable from a previous employer, and he is not on Kota's register.

Nothing stopped this call happening. The parallel with the EdgeTier finding is exact: the control
that should have prevented it is a **scheduling and onboarding control**, not a coaching one.

**Recommend Compliance urgently establish how many unregistered GTM staff are running customer
calls.** The triage shows at least three organisers absent from the register — Mark Fitzgibbon,
Luke Healy, and Tadhg — across roughly a dozen calls in this week alone.
